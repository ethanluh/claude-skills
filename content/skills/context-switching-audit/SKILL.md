# context-switching-audit — flag interleaved domains, recommend batching

## Why this exists

Switching attention between tasks has a measurable cost, not just a
scheduling inconvenience. Per APA's research summary (Meyer et al.),
switch costs come from two sources — reconfiguring mental control
settings for the new task, and residual interference from the task just
left — and can consume up to **40% of otherwise productive time** across
a day of frequent switching. The cost is structural: even fully
predictable, evenly-spaced switches are still measurably slower than
repeating the same task. See
[[apa-multitasking-switch-costs]].

## Checklist

1. **List the day/week's tasks with their domain.** Group into buckets
   (e.g., coursework, research, applications, personal-statement
   drafting, admin). Don't use the plan's own ordering yet — get the raw
   domain tags first.

2. **Count domain transitions per day**, not total tasks. A day with 6
   tasks in one domain has zero switch cost between them; a day
   alternating across 3+ unrelated domains has a real structural cost
   even if the task count is lower.

3. **Flag any day with 3+ unrelated-domain switches** as a structural
   cost, not just a scheduling inconvenience — this is the citable
   threshold from the source note. Name the specific switches (e.g.
   "problem set to research code to essay editing to problem set" — that's
   two more transitions than the buckets require).

4. **Check for two-complex-task pairings.** Only one task in a pair can
   be cognitively demanding for genuine parallelism (e.g., laundry during
   a call) to hold. Two complex/unfamiliar tasks back-to-back are
   sequential switching in disguise — flag these explicitly, since the
   cost is *larger* for complex or unfamiliar tasks than for simple,
   practiced ones.

5. **Recommend consolidation, not just reordering**: propose batching
   same-domain tasks into contiguous blocks (all research-reading
   together, all application-essay editing together), even if that means
   moving tasks across days. State which transitions the batching
   eliminates.

6. **Don't over-correct.** A single domain switch per day, or switching
   into a short, highly habitual task, isn't worth flagging — the source
   notes switching cost is asymmetric and driven by complexity/
   unfamiliarity, not by switching per se. Only flag when transitions are
   frequent (3+) or pair complex/unfamiliar tasks.

7. **State the tradeoff plainly** if full consolidation isn't feasible
   (e.g., a hard deadline forces an out-of-block task) — name the switch
   cost being accepted rather than silently absorbing it into the plan.

## Output format

A short list: flagged day(s), the specific transitions causing the flag,
and a consolidated re-grouping recommendation. Cite the 40%-productivity
figure only once, as the justification, not as a running refrain.
