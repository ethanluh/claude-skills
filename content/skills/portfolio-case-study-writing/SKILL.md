# Portfolio case study writing

## When to draft

- Draft **right after a milestone**, not batched for application season —
  the specific reasoning behind decisions is forgotten by the time you sit
  down later. If the project already shipped and this is a reconstruction,
  say so and flag that some reasoning may be reconstructed rather than
  contemporaneous.
- An abandoned or unsuccessful project is fair material as long as the
  lesson is stated explicitly, not glossed over.

## Structure — Hook / Research / Build / Iterate / Outcome

1. **Hook** — role, methodology, and a preview of the result, in 1-2
   sentences. This is exposition, not a teaser: the reader should already
   know roughly what happened before reading further.
2. **Research** — context before solution. For a client project: who they
   are and the actual brief. For a CS/personal project (no client): what
   gap, need, or curiosity triggered it. Skipping this makes the work read
   as decoration rather than problem-solving.
3. **Build** — what you made and how, but reasoning over description:
   explain *why* a method or tool was chosen, not just that it was used.
4. **Iterate** — the obstacle (conflict) and the turning point (climax):
   a specific thing that didn't work, a key insight, or a pivot. This is
   where self-reflection belongs even for successful projects — and is
   mandatory for unsuccessful ones.
5. **Outcome** — results and lessons. State them plainly; avoid flat
   "we found X" phrasing when the finding was surprising — name the
   surprise without melodrama.

## Non-negotiable rules

- **Results at the top, not the bottom.** Restate the outcome in the Hook
  even though Outcome covers it in full — assume the reader abandons
  partway through.
- **Name your own role explicitly** wherever the project involved anyone
  else (team, advisor, collaborators). Ambiguity here undermines the
  whole write-up's credibility.
- **Brevity per section**: a short paragraph or two, not a novel. Readers
  skim; don't reward skimming with a wall of text.
- **Captions carry real weight.** If there are figures/screenshots/plots,
  captions should let someone reconstruct the project's logic by reading
  only the captions — not just label what's already visible.
- **Text-to-visual ratio**: roughly 60-80% text, 20-40% supporting visuals.
  Too many raw, unannotated screenshots/sketches is a named failure mode —
  clean them up or cut them.
- **Voice**: write in Ethan's own authentic voice, not corporate or
  generic technical-report language. The reader should finish with a
  sense of how he thinks, not just a list of what shipped.

## Common failure modes to check a draft against

- No lessons/self-reflection section, especially on work that didn't
  fully succeed.
- Irrelevant background padding before getting to the actual problem.
- Naming a method or tool without explaining the reasoning behind
  choosing it.
- Results buried at the end instead of previewed in the Hook.
- Own role left ambiguous on a team or advised project.
